# Marcha Content OS

## Brand

**Marcha**

Marcha is a premium, faceless food content brand exploring better-for-you Indian food, sauces, snacks, and global fusion using ingredients available in India.

## Core Positioning

**Modern Indian food ideas for people who want flavour, protein, and clean-label convenience.**

Marcha is not a normal recipe page. It is a food product lab in public.

## Brand Promise

Marcha turns the Indian pantry into modern sauces, snacks, bowls, breakfasts, drinks, desserts, and food products that could actually exist.

## Audience

- Indian Gen Z foodies
- Health-aware young Indians
- Gym/high-protein audience
- Urban quick-commerce shoppers
- People bored of ketchup, mayo, basic chutney, and generic healthy food

## Food Rules

- Vegetarian first
- Egg allowed where useful
- Plant-based variants where possible
- High-protein where natural
- Clean-label direction
- Better-for-you, not diet food
- India-available ingredients only
- Premium supermarket ingredients allowed
- Global ingredients should have Indian substitutes

## Visual Identity

Style:
- Premium editorial
- Minimal food lab
- Indian street-food modernized
- Hard-flash commercial photography
- Close-up food action
- Faceless hands
- Crisp shadows
- Solid matte backgrounds
- High-saturation color blocking
- Deep focus and sharp edges
- 1970s hard-flash cookbook meets modern pop-art food lab
- Large negative space for Paquito typography

Image rules:
- Generate visuals without text, logos, labels, or watermarks.
- Final carousel visuals should remain clean; the user adds text manually using the provided placement layouts.
- Provide a separate text placement layout for every slide.
- Text should be integrated into the composition, not placed in separate blocks.
- Prefer editorial negative-space typography, thin callout lines, menu-style microcopy, and small page numbers.
- Avoid sticker-like labels, big CTA buttons, and floating filled text cards.
- Use food hero shots, ingredient layouts, sauce pours, dipping actions, and hand gestures.
- Use only ingredients available in India.
- Keep everything safe, edible, vegetarian-first, and product-believable.
- Avoid cluttered kitchens, rustic styling, soft-focus food, smoky backgrounds, and AI-looking typography.
- Every slide visual must be highly relevant to the slide copy.
- Every cover must have one instantly readable scroll-stopping visual action or food object.
- Every exported asset must pass the locked pixel-size check for its format.

## AI Position

AI stays mostly behind the scenes.

Public language:
- “food concept”
- “future Indian food idea”
- “test kitchen concept”
- “should this exist?”

Avoid making the page feel like an AI gimmick.

## Research Gate

No Marcha concept should be created from imagination alone.

Before creating a new concept, check current market and internet signals:
- Indian food and FMCG trends
- quick-commerce food behaviour
- Gen Z food and snack behaviour
- sauce, condiment, dip, and chutney launches
- high-protein, clean-label, low-sugar, functional, and better-for-you trends
- restaurant/menu formats that are travelling online
- social formats that are gaining saves, comments, and shares

Use research to decide:
- whether the idea has cultural relevance
- whether the flavour is familiar enough for India
- whether the format feels new enough to post
- whether the concept could become a real product
- whether the audience would save, try, or buy it
- whether the visual can compete in-feed within one second
- whether the idea has enough novelty without becoming fake or gimmicky

The output should mention the trend logic briefly, without making the post feel like a research report.

Research sources should include, when useful:
- current food trend reporting
- social/platform behaviour
- Indian FMCG and quick-commerce signals
- restaurant/menu signals
- category/product launches
- local ingredient availability

## Realism Gate

Every Marcha food visual must look as if the food was actually made in a studio.

Rules:
- realistic texture, colour, gloss, density, and portion size
- realistic shape, structure, and ingredient-specific behaviour
- sauce viscosity should match the ingredients
- viscosity should be named before prompting: spoonable, pourable ribbon, slow drip, spreadable, set, airy, crumbly, crunchy, or crisp
- fried, roasted, blended, creamy, crunchy, or fresh elements should look physically believable
- Indian ingredients should look like their real-world versions
- no impossible plating, fake-perfect food geometry, plastic-looking sauces, or fantasy colours
- if a concept uses a sauce, define its likely texture before generating the image
- if a concept uses fusion, define the real preparation logic before generating the image

For image prompts, always include food-state details:
- thick / pourable / glossy / chunky / creamy / roasted / charred / crisp / fresh
- actual colour notes such as deep tomato red, Kashmiri chilli orange-red, pale makhani beige, herb green
- realistic shape notes such as smooth dome, torn edge, folded roll, spoon ridge, coarse crumble, crisp blister, or slow drip
- realistic surface details such as oil sheen, spice flecks, seed texture, herb garnish, toasted edges, grain size, bubbles, separated oil, char, or crumb

If the food would not look edible in real life, do not generate it.

## Creative Quality Gate

Before delivery, score each concept and slide direction internally against:
- scroll-stop power
- copy-to-visual relevance
- ingredient realism
- product potential
- Indian market fit
- brand distinctiveness
- save/share/comment potential

If any slide is visually generic, weakly related to the copy, or not premium enough, revise the prompt before final delivery.

## Format Mix

5 posts per week:

- 2 carousels
- 2 AI motion reels
- 1 single image

Monthly:

- 8 carousels
- 8 reels
- 4 single images

## Carousel Format

7 slides:

1. Hook cover
2. The idea
3. Ingredients
4. Making/action
5. Use cases
6. Why it works
7. CTA

Carousel output package:
- 7 clean visual slides with no text
- 7 matching text placement layout samples
- exact slide-by-slide copy
- caption and CTA
- research logic and realism notes

Carousel visual rhythm:
- Slide 1: close-up food action on the pillar's assigned single-colour background with editorial headline in negative space
- Slide 2: concept/product logic as a clean menu-page layout
- Slide 3: ingredient still life with thin callout labels
- Slide 4: faceless hand action with short integrated process copy
- Slide 5: use-case food grid or hero usage shot with small captions
- Slide 6: simple framework or flavour logic using lines, not cards
- Slide 7: premium CTA poster with one question and one small response line

## Reel Format

AI motion clips:

1. 1-second hook
2. food/action reveal
3. ingredient flash
4. pour/dip/drizzle
5. final concept
6. question CTA

Reel visual rhythm:
- Use 9:16 frames only.
- Keep food motion central and text in safe zones.
- Best motions: sauce pour, dip, drizzle, sprinkle, tear, spread, chopstick lift, spoon swirl.
- Use 3-7 word overlays only.

## Single Image Format

Use for:

- food hero shot
- quote/hot take
- mock packaging
- product concept poster

Single image visual rhythm:
- Use 4:5 only.
- One idea per post.
- One big line or one product name.
- Use a strong food object, a hard shadow, and a clean field of color.
